Plainchant Scribe

Make the book. Keep the files.

An EPUB studio that will let you write, lay out, check, and export a book from your own XHTML, CSS, and package files, without a tool rewriting them behind your back.

Open the studio Read the manual

I.The idea

An EPUB is a zip of ordinary files: chapters in XHTML, a stylesheet, images, fonts, and a package file that lists them and puts them in order. Most book tools keep their own model of your book and write the files out when you save. Scribe will not.

In Scribe the files are the book. Every change you make, on the page or in a form, edits only the characters it has to. Your formatting, your comments, and the parts of the file you did not touch stay exactly as you wrote them. Export zips the files into an EPUB.

❦

II.The plan

The work arrives in this order. Each step is useful on its own.

  1. The book format. Start a book, and read and edit its package file without disturbing it.
  2. A reading view. See a chapter as a reader would: paged or scrolled, light or dark, on a phone, an e-ink reader, or a tablet.
  3. Structure. Metadata, cover, reading order, and a contents page built from your headings.
  4. Layout. Typography, chapter openers, scene breaks, footnotes, and embedded fonts, all written as plain CSS you can read.
  5. Import, export, and checks. Open an existing EPUB or Markdown, export a valid book, and find problems before a store does.
  6. Accessibility and stores. Accessibility metadata, and a checklist for each store.

Scribe will not be a Markdown writing app, will not make Kindle-only formats (Amazon takes EPUB), and will not support scripted or interactive books.

❦

III.Where it stands today

The studio you can open now makes the files of a new EPUB book, or opens a short example book, and shows each chapter beside the code the way a reading system would. It edits ordinary files without rewriting them and works offline. It cannot export an EPUB yet.

No account, no tracking. Your work stays on your device. The only network feature is an optional one that you switch on yourself, to send a book to a reader on your own network.
